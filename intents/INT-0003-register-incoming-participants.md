# INT-0003: Register Incoming Participants

**Minted by steward zen, 2026-09-05:** *"I vote we allow any registration,
restricted by invitation into the private repo. Then their open delegation to
begin working. This might become the first multi-steward instance so it is
better to be open first then limit later than restrictive now and find a lot
of friction."*

## Purpose

Give anyone who wants to work in this house a short path in, and give them
real work to do when they arrive.

## The posture, stated plainly

**Open first. Limit later, if lived load shows a reason.**

Registration is not a filter. Anyone who asks may register, and registration
carries a standing delegation that lets them begin — no waiting for a bespoke
commission, no queue behind the steward.

The boundary that does the work is **the invitation to this private
repository**. That is a deliberate choice about where the gate sits: at access,
which the steward controls directly and can withdraw, rather than at standing,
which would mean judging people before they have done anything.

This is recoverable in the direction it is likely to fail. Rules can be added
when something actually goes wrong. A teacher who hits friction on their first
day usually does not come back for a second.

## In scope

- A one-page arrival path: read, register, add an author-map row, begin.
- Participant records under `participants/`, holding the minimum needed to
  attribute work — no personnel files.
- The **open standing delegation** in [WS-0003](../workspaces/WS-0003-arrivals/workspace.md),
  under which any registered participant may work without a further
  commission.
- Growth into **Steward of Scope** (RFC-0006 §5, tier 3) when someone is
  carrying an area in practice.

## Out of scope

- Registering anyone who has not knowingly agreed. Attendance at the INSET is
  not registration; nobody is enrolled by having been in the room.
- Personal data about learners, parents, or staff in any participant record.
- Constitutional amendment, publication, or making this repository public.
- Speaking for San Jose Central School or DepEd Tacloban.

## What this intent does not pretend

Two things are worth stating so a later reader is not misled.

**Registration and repository access are different, and here they are
deliberately joined.** RFC-0006's first principle is that capability is not
consent — usually invoked to stop capability implying standing. This house
runs the pairing in the other direction: standing is open, and access is the
gate. That is a stewardship choice about where friction belongs, not a claim
that the two are the same thing.

**Branch protection is not available on this repository's plan.** Any
collaborator can therefore push to `main` directly, and no machinery prevents
it. Subok raised this before the posture was set; the steward considered it and
chose openness anyway. It is recorded here as an accepted risk rather than an
oversight, and it is the first thing to revisit if the house grows.

## On becoming a multi-steward house

The steward's reason for the open posture is that this may be the first ZenOS
instance with more than one steward. Two different things go by that name and
the constitution treats them differently.

**Steward of Scope** — RFC-0006 §5, tier 3 — holds merge and approval rights
over a named area, recorded as a standing delegation naming the scope. There
may be several, and **no graduation is required**. If a teacher ends up
carrying the Grade 4 mathematics lesson bank, that is the tier for it, and it
is available in this house today.

**Constitutional Steward** — tier 4 — is a different threshold. RFC-0006 §8
ends single-steward mode only when an ecosystem has **at least three**
constitutional stewards, at which point a quorum rule is adopted by decision
record. That is not reached by wanting it, and this intent does not begin it.

The realistic first step toward the steward's ambition is therefore a second
and third **Steward of Scope** here — which this intent makes reachable
without amending anything.

## Success conditions

1. Someone who wants to help can register and start the same day.
2. Every contribution traces to a named participant through the author map.
3. Nobody is registered who did not ask to be.
4. When someone outgrows the open delegation, the path upward already exists
   and is recorded rather than improvised.

## Review conditions

Return this posture to steward judgment if any of the following happens:

- a direct push to `main` bypasses review;
- a participant record accumulates personal data it does not need;
- the open delegation is used for something it plainly does not cover; or
- registrations arrive from people who did not ask.

None of these are predictions. They are the conditions under which
*limit later* stops being a plan and becomes the work.
