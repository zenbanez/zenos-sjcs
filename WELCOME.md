# Welcome — you are about to start a house

You have a folder full of plain text files. That is the whole system. There is
no hidden operating database: if you can open the repository and read it, you
can investigate how the house works.

This welcome is for **you, the human steward**. [ONBOARDING.md](ONBOARDING.md)
contains the setup mechanics, while [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) tells intelligent
participants how to arrive and work here.

> The repository is the truth. This welcome is just the door.

## What a house is, in one breath

A house is a place where you and one or more intelligent participants do real
work together and leave an honest trail of it. You bring **intent and
judgment**. Participants bring **perspective, execution, and scale**. The files
between you preserve the relationships that make the work intelligible.

The operating loop is shown in [arrival-infographic.svg](arrival-infographic.svg):

**Intent → Workspace → Delegation → Action → Evidence → Knowledge → Decision → a wiser Intent**

You do not need to memorize that loop before beginning.

## Your job is small, and it remains yours

Most work can be delegated. Five judgments remain with the human steward:

1. **Name new work.** You mint the top-level identifier for a new intent.
2. **Set scope and limits.** Say what the work is for and what it must not do.
3. **Grant permission.** Capability is not consent; work proceeds through a
   delegation you approve.
4. **Confirm knowledge.** Participants may propose lessons; you decide what the
   house adopts as knowledge.
5. **Decide when work is done.** Completion, archival, adoption, and departure
   into another house remain steward judgments.

The guard does not replace your judgment. It helps the repository return work
whose required relationships are missing.

## How to begin with an agent beside you

You do not have to write every file by hand. The ordinary pattern is simple:
you decide, the agent drafts, and you review.

1. **Say why.** Describe the first thing you want the house to pursue.
2. **Name it.** Mint the first intent when its purpose and limits are clear.
3. **Open a room.** Ask the agent to draft a workspace for that intent.
4. **Delegate one bounded task.** Make both permission and prohibition visible.
5. **Review the return.** Inspect the change, evidence, warrant trail, lessons,
   and remaining questions.

That is one turn of the loop. The house becomes real through the relationships
you establish, not through the amount of machinery you activate.

If anything seems to move without your say-so, stop and ask for the authority
trail. The house is designed to make that question answerable.
