# Prompts to begin — a steward's phrasebook

These phrases are optional guidance, not commands embedded in the repository
and not grants of authority. Say things in your own voice. The intent,
delegation, steward approval, and committed record—not the wording of a
prompt—determine what belongs.

If this house is still uninstantiated, begin with [ONBOARDING.md](ONBOARDING.md)
so the initial human steward and Git identity are visible first.

## 1. Bring an agent in

> “Please read AGENTS.md and orient yourself with this house.”

> “Propose your participant registration and first warrant for my review.”

Ask the agent to identify the intent and delegation before it makes a direct
commit. Registration records identity; it does not create unlimited standing.

## 2. Begin the first operating loop

> “I want this house to ______. Please draft a bounded intent for that.”

After reviewing the draft, **you** mint its ID:

> “I mint this as INT-0001.”

Then continue:

> “Draft a workspace for INT-0001 with objectives, limits, and open questions.”

> “Draft a delegation for ______. State clearly what it may and may not do.”

> “I approve that delegation. Proceed within those bounds.”

> “Show me what changed, what evidence supports it, what you learned, and what
> still requires my judgment.”

That return is one complete turn of the house.

## 3. Steer by asking

> “Does this align with our current intent?”

> “Is this within your delegation? Show me the source record.”

> “Propose it; do not act yet.”

> “Not quite. Revise this part: ______.”

> “Keep that as a knowledge candidate. I have not promoted it.”

> “Stop. That requires my authorization.”

These questions restore the work to its relationships instead of turning every
course correction into a restart.

## 4. The steward-only moments

- **Name work:** “Mint that as INT-0002.”
- **Set limits:** “Add a bound: it may read, but it may not send.”
- **Grant permission:** “Yes, proceed within D-002.”
- **Confirm knowledge:** “I promote that candidate to knowledge.”
- **Decide completion:** “Mark the intent complete and prepare the required
  distillation before archival.”

## 5. Return after time away

> “Catch me up. What changed since I last looked, what is waiting on me, and
> which source records should I inspect?”

> “Which judgments are held, and why was each one held?”

Ask before steering. Stale assumptions are more dangerous than admitting you
need orientation.

## 6. When you do not know what comes next

> “What is the smallest meaningful next step that fits our active intent and
> current delegation?”

The agent may propose. You still decide.
