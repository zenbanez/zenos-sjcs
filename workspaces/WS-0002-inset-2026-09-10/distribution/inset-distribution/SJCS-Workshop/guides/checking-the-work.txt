===========================================================
  CHECKING THE WORK
===========================================================

The AI will give you something that looks finished.

Looking finished and being usable are different things.
This page is how you tell them apart. It takes about two
minutes per output.

You are not checking whether the AI did a good job.
You are deciding whether YOU will put your name on it.


-----------------------------------------------------------
THE SEVEN QUESTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------

  1. Does it match our curriculum?

     Check the competency against the guide in the
     references folder. The AI can produce a beautiful
     lesson for the wrong competency.


  2. Will it fit the period?

     Add up the timings. AI plans are almost always too
     long. A 60-minute plan that needs 90 minutes is not
     a plan, it is a wish.


  3. Do we actually have the materials?

     Cross out anything requiring equipment, printing
     budget, or internet the school does not have.


  4. Is the reading level right?

     Not right for Grade 5 in general. Right for YOUR
     Grade 5, the one sitting in front of you in
     September.


  5. Is anything simply wrong?

     Check facts, dates, examples, and any computation.
     The AI states wrong things with the same confidence
     it states right ones. It does not warn you.


  6. Is it local?

     Generic examples about snow, subways, and dollars
     teach less than examples about our own barangay,
     our own prices, our own names. Ask it to change
     them.


  7. What does it not know?

     This is the one that only you can answer.

     What do you know about this class, this week, this
     topic, that never appeared anywhere in what you
     typed? The learner who lost a parent last month.
     The section that had no class Tuesday because of
     the storm. The three who share one textbook.

     Put that in yourself - in the lesson, as you review
     it. Not into the AI.

     Keep names out of what you type. You can adapt a
     lesson for a grieving child without ever telling a
     machine which child it is. That is the part of
     teaching that does not come out of a machine, and it
     is also the part that should not be typed into one.


-----------------------------------------------------------
A GOOD HABIT
-----------------------------------------------------------

When something is wrong, do not start over.

Tell it what is wrong in ordinary words:

    "This is too long for a 50-minute period. Cut it."

    "We have no projector. Redo the activities."

    "The examples are too foreign. Use local ones."

    "My learners cannot read at this level yet."

You will get further in three rounds of that than in
thirty minutes of trying to write the perfect first
question.


-----------------------------------------------------------
THE TEST THAT MATTERS
-----------------------------------------------------------

Before you save it, ask yourself one thing:

    Would I be comfortable if a fellow teacher opened
    this next year and taught from it without asking
    me anything?

    And: does it name any learner or parent? If it does,
    take the name out before you hand it in.

If yes, save it. Someone probably will.

If no, it is not finished - and now you know exactly
what to fix.
