# Daily Lesson Log (DLL) and Detailed Lesson Plan (DLP) — official blank templates

Transcribed directly from the scanned official PDF, `DO_s2016_042.pdf` (retained
alongside this file), for a plain, AI-readable version of the same forms. Field
labels and structure are reproduced verbatim from the enclosure's Annex 1A and
Annex 1B; explanatory sentences below each part are this document's own
paraphrase of the enclosure's numbered guidance (paragraphs 40–55), for
orientation, not a substitute for reading the source PDF directly.

## Who uses which

Per the order's own §H (paragraph 46): **newly-hired teachers without
professional teaching experience** must prepare a daily DLP for a year.
Newly-hired teachers rated "Very Satisfactory" or "Outstanding" in their RPMS
are no longer required to; those rated "Satisfactory" still are, until their
RPMS assessment improves. **Whenever new content is integrated into the
curriculum, all teachers — regardless of experience — are required to write a
DLP for that content.** Everyone else uses the DLL. This is worth surfacing to
INSET participants explicitly: the workshop's own materials currently assume
DLL for everyone.

## Annex 1B — Daily Lesson Log, Grades 1 to 12

**Header fields:** School | Grade Level | Teacher | Learning Area | Teaching
Dates and Time | Quarter

A single week (Monday–Friday) in one table. Weekly, not daily — one DLL
covers everything taught that week for one Grade/Learning Area.

**I. OBJECTIVES** — must be met over the week and connected to curriculum
standards. Weekly objectives shall be derived from the Curriculum Guide (CG).
  - A. Content Standards
  - B. Performance Standards
  - C. Learning Competencies / Objectives — write the LC code for each

**II. CONTENT** — what the lesson is about; the subject matter the teacher
aims to teach. In the CG this can span a week or two.

**III. LEARNING RESOURCES** — list materials used on each day; a mix of
concrete/manipulative and paper-based materials sustains interest.
  - A. References
    1. Teacher's Guide pages
    2. Learner's Materials pages
    3. Textbook pages
    4. Additional Materials from Learning Resource (LR) portal
  - B. Other Learning Resources

**IV. PROCEDURES** — spread appropriately across the week; always guided by
demonstration of learning inferred from formative assessment. Indicate time
allotment for each step.
  - A. Reviewing previous lesson or presenting the new lesson
  - B. Establishing a purpose for the lesson
  - C. Presenting examples/instances of the new lesson
  - D. Discussing new concepts and practicing new skills #1
  - E. Discussing new concepts and practicing new skills #2
  - F. Developing mastery *(leads to Formative Assessment 3)*
  - G. Finding practical applications of concepts and skills in daily living
  - H. Making generalizations and abstractions about the lesson
  - I. Evaluating learning
  - J. Additional activities for application or remediation

**V. REMARKS** — one row, freeform: continuation to the next day due to
re-teaching, insufficient time, class suspension, etc.

**VI. REFLECTION** — freeform per item, filled after delivery:
  - A. No. of learners who earned 80% in the evaluation
  - B. No. of learners who require additional activities for remediation
  - C. Did the remedial lessons work? No. of learners who caught up
  - D. No. of learners who continue to require remediation
  - E. Which of my teaching strategies worked well? Why did these work?
  - F. What difficulties did I encounter which my principal or supervisor can
     help me solve?
  - G. What innovation or localized materials did I use/discover which I wish
     to share with other teachers?

*Feedback on the official form: bld.tld@deped.gov.ph*

A Filipino-language version of this identical form exists as Annex 1C
("Pang-araw-araw na Tala sa Pagtuturo") — same fields, translated. Not
transcribed here; see the retained PDF if needed.

## Annex 1A — Kindergarten Daily Lesson Log

**Header fields:** School | Grade Level | Teacher | Learning Area | Teaching
Dates and Time | Quarter

Structured differently from Grades 1–12: rows are **blocks of time**, not
lesson-procedure steps, because a Kindergarten day is organized by routine
rather than subject periods. Each block below (except Nap Time and the final
Dismissal Routine) repeats the same four objective sub-fields, filled per day
Monday–Friday:

> *Developmental Domain(s): _____*
> *Content Standard: _____*
> *Performance Standard: _____*
> *Learning Competency Code: _____*

**Blocks of time, in order:**

1. Arrival Time
2. Meeting Time 1
3. Work Period 1
4. Meeting Time 2
5. Supervised Recess
6. Nap Time *(no objective sub-fields — a single row)*
7. Story
8. Work Period 2
9. Indoor/Outdoor Play
10. Meeting Time 3 *(= Dismissal Routine — no objective sub-fields)*

Followed by the same **Remarks** and **Reflection (A–G)** sections as Annex
1B above, worded identically.

## Detailed Lesson Plan (DLP) — same six parts, written in full

The DLP is **not** a separate table. Per §H (paragraphs 47–55), it shares the
DLL's exact six components — **Objectives, Content, Learning Resources,
Procedures, Remarks, Reflection** — but for a *single day's* lesson, written
out as full narrative/prose rather than compressed into weekly table cells.

- **Objectives, Content, Learning Resources:** same meaning as in the DLL,
  above.
- **Procedures:** the same ten-part structure (Before/During/After the
  Lesson), written as full sentences describing what the teacher and learners
  will actually do and say, with time allotments. Flexibility is explicitly
  permitted — "changes in the procedure are allowed based on time constraints
  or when adjustments in teaching are needed." Assignments/homework are
  optional post-lesson formative assessment, per DepEd Memorandum 329, s.
  2010, and mandatory-optional status applies at all grade levels.
- **Remarks:** documents specific instances requiring lesson continuation to
  the next day (re-teaching, insufficient time, class suspension).
- **Reflection:** filled out right after the lesson is delivered — same
  spirit as the DLL's, focused on what worked, what needs improvement, and
  what could be adjusted next time.

No blank DLP form/table exists in the scanned enclosure to reproduce — its
format is deliberately prose, not tabular. A teacher required to submit a DLP
should follow the DLL's six-part structure above, expanded into full written
form for one day rather than five.
